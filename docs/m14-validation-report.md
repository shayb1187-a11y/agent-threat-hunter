## flaws.cloud: what a real CloudTrail trail did to ATH

Source: `reports/m14/flaws_cloud.json`. 1,939,207 management events, 1,242 distinct
API names, 2017-02-12 to 2020-10-07, one account, two regions.

**E0 ingestion.** The adapter maps 4 authentication and 5 management event names. Before
step 1 it kept 1.13% of the trail; the `invokedBy` principal fallback (service-invoked
`AssumeRole`, 57,616 records that were being dropped as "no usable principal") lifted that
to 4.10%. The remaining 95.9% is 1,233 event names with no canonical home. `RunInstances`
alone is 1,323,105 records, almost all `RequestLimitExceeded` or `UnauthorizedOperation`
failures from CTF players trying to mine on the account's dime. ATH sees none of it, and
`python main.py visibility` says so: `cloud_management_activity` is "available" on 96
rows.

**E1 analyst load.** 39 findings in 3.7 years. 36 are ATH-005 (failed-logon burst), 3 are
AWS-002 (trail stopped or deleted). Correlation formed 39 cases with 0 links: every finding
is a singleton, every case `confidence=low`. The triage layer marked all 39
`likely_malicious` with score 0, by the `graded_high_by_detection` veto, because every
finding on this dataset is HIGH or CRITICAL. Triage reduction: 0%.

**What that means.** Two things, neither flattering:

1. *The benign layer has no purchase on cloud telemetry.* It defers to the rule's own grade
   at HIGH and above, and every cloud rule grades HIGH and above. On this data the layer
   built to reduce analyst load could not have reduced it by design.
2. *ATH-005 does not require the success it is named after.* 35 of 36 findings say "no
   successful logon was observed" and are still HIGH. One is an 11-role `AssumeRole`
   enumeration in 10 seconds by `Level6` from one address: real reconnaissance, correctly
   surfaced, but graded identically to a burst that was followed by a success. Whether a
   failed burst deserves HIGH is a design question; the answer here is that 35 of them
   over 3.7 years is not an unmanageable load, and that severity carries no information
   about outcome.

**AWS-001 never fired** despite 32 `AttachUserPolicy` and 53 `CreateAccessKey` records. 71
of the 96 management rows are denied attempts. The allowed ones do not form a policy-attach
followed by key-creation for one identity inside the rule's window. Either the chain does
not exist in this account or the window misses it; the per-identity timeline is a step-5
item.

**E5 singleton gate.** All 39 findings are HIGH or above, so all 39 would be investigated.
The gate that drops lone MEDIUM findings had nothing to drop here; it has not yet been
tested on a dataset with medium-severity rules firing.

**E6 crew.** `platforms=aws`; identity, attack and control_plane stand up; endpoint and
network are excluded for missing channels. Correct.

**E8 scaling** (time-ordered prefixes of the ingested data):

| Fraction | Events | Findings | Cases | Hunt s | Environment s | Correlate s |
|----------|--------|----------|-------|--------|---------------|-------------|
| 0.10 | 8,528 | 1 | 1 | 0.13 | 0.28 | 0.003 |
| 0.25 | 16,825 | 2 | 2 | 0.15 | 0.47 | 0.004 |
| 0.50 | 41,253 | 29 | 29 | 2.29 | 1.17 | 0.031 |
| 1.00 | 79,520 | 39 | 39 | 3.39 | 2.60 | 0.039 |

Hunt and environment are roughly linear in events. Correlation is negligible at 39 findings
and its pairwise cost has not been exercised; the safety cap stays at 200k events until a
dataset produces hundreds of findings.

**Labels.** None. This row cannot carry precision, recall or investigation quality. It was
never meant to; its job was to put a real trail through the front door and count what fell
out.

## Kubernetes: two real apiserver logs, and three things the fixture had hidden

Sources: `reports/m14/k8s_ci.json` (Kubernetes CI e2e run, kube-apiserver 1.37, 38
minutes, 213 principals), `reports/m14/k8ntext.json` (K8NTEXT, kubeadm 1.28-1.30, one human
administrator, 56 days). Both benign. Before-fix rows are kept beside them as
`*.before-exec-fix.json`.

**Three adapter defects, all found in the first hour of real data, none visible on the
project's own fixture:**

1. **Real apiservers log `kubectl exec` as verb `get`.** The exec is a protocol-upgrade
   request; both clusters recorded it as `get` (K8NTEXT half `get`, half `create`
   depending on the client version). The adapter mapped `create`/`connect` only: 0 of
   5,962 execs in the CI run were ingested and K8S-002 was structurally blind on any real
   cluster. Fixed in the adapter: the exec row now carries the canonical verb `exec`
   whatever the apiserver wrote, mirroring how the CloudTrail adapter already turns
   `AttachUserPolicy` into `attach`; K8S-002 matches on `exec`. The KQL note was updated.
2. **A successful exec answers `101 Switching Protocols`, not `200`.** The decision logic
   accepted only 2xx, so every successful shell into a container would have been recorded
   as *denied*. Fixed: 101 is `allowed`.
3. **The fixture wrote the audit username into the RBAC subject.** A real binding names a
   service account as `{kind: ServiceAccount, name: ci-runner, namespace: ci}`; the audit
   log later names the same account `system:serviceaccount:ci:ci-runner`. The fixture had
   put the second spelling into the subject, which no cluster does, so K8S-002's join of
   grant target to exec actor worked on the fixture and could never work on real data.
   Fixed in the adapter (subjects are canonicalised to the username form) and the fixture
   was corrected to the real shape, with a test that it stays that way.

All three are normalization, not detection. No rule threshold or benign signal changed.

**E0 ingestion.** CI run: 267,979 completed requests, 7,951 kept (2.97%): 1,989 RBAC
binding creates, every one with a request body, and 5,962 execs, all by the test harness
user `kubecfg`. Dropped: 29,742 `get` on pods, 11,199 pod status patches, 9,938 non-resource
requests, and so on down a long tail; `serviceaccounts/token` (2,421) and `secrets`
operations (3,329) have no canonical home and are reported as unmapped. K8NTEXT: 31 of
18,448 kept.

**E1 analyst load, CI run.** 55 K8S-001 findings in 38 minutes. All 55 are `cluster-admin`
grants: 54 to the `default` service account of a freshly created per-test namespace
(`volumemode-2343`, `ephemeral-5365`, ...) and one to `system:masters` at bootstrap. Every
one is legitimate e2e test scaffolding, every one is a singleton case at `confidence=low`,
and triage kept all 55 (`graded_high_by_detection` again). Extrapolated, that is **2,070
findings per day** from a cluster doing nothing wrong. The other 1,934 binding creates
(mostly `e2e-test-privileged-psp` and `extension-apiserver-authentication-reader`) were
correctly left alone: K8S-001 is precise about *which* role, and useless about *whether
the grant is normal here*. K8S-002 produced 0 findings: no grantee exec'd within the
window, so the exec-after-grant chain did not fire on 5,962 execs. That is the correct
outcome and the first evidence the rule is quiet under load.

**E1, K8NTEXT.** 13 binding creates, none to a maximally privileged role; 18 human execs
with no preceding grant. 0 findings. Also correct.

**E6 crew and environment.** `platforms=kubernetes`; control_plane, identity and attack
stand up. But the environment model reports **0 identities** for a cluster with 213
distinct principals: it derives identities from the logon and process tables only, and
control-plane actors are not counted. That is a real gap in the environment model, recorded
here for its own milestone rather than patched mid-validation.

**E8.** Hunt 0.3 s on 7,951 events; correlation 0.05 s at 55 findings.

**What is blocked.** Injected attacks (plan 5.3) need a `kind` cluster and Stratus Red
Team; this machine has no Docker. Until they run on a host that does, the Kubernetes rows
are benign-only: they measure false-positive load and coverage, and carry no recall or
investigation figure.

## DEDALE: the first labelled Windows incident, and the first clean miss

Sources: `reports/m14/dedale_probe_hours.json` (profile) and
`reports/m14/dedale_probe_hours.deterministic.json` (incident). Two hours of a 30-client
Active Directory estate: 2024-12-25T15 (benign week 1) and 2025-01-06T10 (the hour the
APT starts on CLIENT2). Labels: `data/external/dedale/labels/dedale_class1_labels.json`,
9,567 native refs converted from DEDALE's class-1 label files by `scripts/dedale_labels.py`.

**E0 ingestion.** 1,383,837 Winlogbeat events read; 6,580 kept (0.48%): 5,934 Sysmon
process creates and 646 Security logons. Unmapped, by class: Sysmon 11 file-create
395,009; Sysmon 26 file-delete 339,036; Sysmon 7 image-load 331,881; Sysmon 10
process-access 230,887; Sysmon 22 DNS 52,073; Security 4672/4634, PowerShell 4103/4104.
There is no Sysmon 3 in the data at all, so the network table is empty and the network
specialist is excluded by crew assembly. 30 hosts and 57 identities are recognised;
`platforms=windows`.

**What the labels can and cannot credit.** DEDALE labels 9,567 host events as the attack
on the two client machines. 84 of them are process creates and 9,483 are file, image-load,
pipe, process-access and PowerShell-provider events with no canonical table: **99.1% of the
labelled attack activity is in channels ATH does not ingest.** No Security-channel event is
labelled, so a logon-based detection of the lateral movement (ATH-005/006/007 on 4624s)
would be scored as noise by these labels. Both facts are carried into the table as the
`unresolved` count and stated here rather than adjusted for.

**E2 on the attack-start hour.** 3 labelled process creates resolved in the hour:
`powershell.exe` spawning `scvhost.exe` from `AppData\Roaming` at 10:03:44, then
`svcmon.exe` from `AppData\Local\Temp` with a C2 URL on its command line. **0 findings.
Not detected. No case, no investigation, 0 of 2 conclusions.** The chain that ATH's rule
set was built around (Office parent, encoded PowerShell, LSASS, PsExec-style service
execution) is not what this attack does in its first hour: LibreOffice opened the
document, the PowerShell was a plain `Invoke-WebRequest` with `-ExecutionPolicy Bypass`,
and the implant is an unsigned binary with a system-looking name in a user-writable
directory. Nothing in the twelve endpoint rules describes "unknown executable from
`AppData` with a masquerading name" (T1036) or "PowerShell downloading and starting a
file" without encoding. That is the finding, and it is the one this milestone exists to
produce: on a foreign intrusion the rule set is precise and narrow, and narrow lost.

The remaining labelled days carry behaviour ATH does describe: `cmd.exe` discovery
sequences under one parent (ATH-010), Run-key persistence, `taskkill`, `xcopy` to a
share, impacket-driven lateral movement to CLIENT1 that should produce `services.exe`
children (ATH-007) and type-3 logons from CLIENT2 (ATH-006). Whether any of it fires is the
next measurement, not a prediction.

**LLM arm.** Requested and not run: there is no API key on this machine. The record says
"unavailable" rather than silently reporting the deterministic arm twice.

**Adapter.** `ath.telemetry.winlogbeat_source` reads ECS NDJSON (plain, bz2, and the
zip-deflated bz2 the archive members are stored as); maps Sysmon 1 / 3 and Security
4624 / 4625; counts every other channel per class in
`SourceLoadResult.unmapped` instead of one issue object per row (645,000 an hour).
`signature_status` is `unknown` on every process row: Sysmon's version-info `Company` is
what the binary says about itself, not a signature, and the known-security-tool signal in
triage cannot engage. Tests: `tests/test_winlogbeat_source.py`.

## DEDALE, full days: the Windows false-positive load and the miss, both explained

Sources: `reports/m14/dedale_d03.json`, `reports/m14/dedale_d15.json`,
`reports/m14/dedale_d15.deterministic.json`; per-hour denominators in
`data/external/dedale/hour_stats.json`. Each day has 11 active hours (the estate is idle
overnight); the fetch filtered 9.3M raw events per day to the channels ATH can use.

**E1, benign day.** 30 findings, all ATH-004 ("possible LSASS credential access"), all
HIGH, all singleton cases, all kept by triage. Every one is `lsass.exe` itself, started by
`wininit.exe` at 08:0x when each of the 30 VMs boots: the rule's "explicit lsass
reference" pattern matches the image path of the process it exists to protect. That is
67.5 false positives per day from an estate doing nothing, and it is a detection-precision
defect (the event is representable and the rule fires on it wrongly), compounded by the
triage veto at HIGH. Everything else stayed silent across 43,994 process creates and
14,285 logons, which on a benign day is the correct outcome for the other eleven endpoint
rules.

**E2, attack day.** The same 30 boot findings and nothing else. Five labelled process
creates were resolved: `powershell.exe` spawning `scvhost.exe` from `AppData\Roaming`,
`svcmon.exe` twice from `AppData\Local\Temp` with `https://172.19.0.2:443` on its command
line, and `cmd.exe` running `reg add HKCU\...\Run`. None surfaced. Recall 0/5. Case
precision 0%: 30 cases, all noise. The investigated case was one of the false positives;
its three facts and two inferences were correct and cited, and one of them named `CLIENT2`,
which satisfied a `must_conclude` item by coincidence. A hostname is too weak a rubric
item and this hit is discounted.

**Why the implant was missed, by class.**

- *Representation.* The PowerShell that fetched the implant has no Sysmon process-create
  record on CLIENT2 (Sysmon does log PowerShell here: 60 creates across the estate that
  day). It exists only in the PowerShell provider channels (600/800/4103/4104), which have
  no canonical table; `python main.py visibility` already lists `script_block` as
  `absent_by_schema`. The document was opened by LibreOffice, which is not in
  `OFFICE_APPLICATIONS`. 9,562 of 9,567 labelled events are file, image-load,
  process-access, pipe and DNS events with no table.
- *Detection coverage.* `scvhost.exe` and `svcmon.exe` are unsigned binaries with
  system-looking names running from user-writable directories (T1036), and the Run-key
  write is textbook persistence (T1547.001). Both are fully representable in the process
  table today. No rule describes either.

**E8.** Hunt is 16.8 s on 58k rows and scales roughly linearly; the cost is the
command-line regex rules (ATH-011 4.9 s, ATH-004 4.6 s, ATH-008 3.7 s, ATH-012 2.6 s), not
correlation (0.7 s at 30 findings). The 200k-event cap remains an engineering cap; nothing
measured argues for raising or lowering it yet.

## Conclusion: what the evidence says the next problem is

Across every dataset the dominant failure is the same one, and it is not the agent.

| Failure class | Where it was measured | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| **Representation** | flaws.cloud 95.9% unmapped; Kubernetes 97-99.8% unmapped incl. `serviceaccounts/token` and `secrets`; DEDALE 99.4% of raw events and 99.9% of *labelled attack events* unrepresentable; the initial-access PowerShell visible only in a channel with no table; no Sysmon 3 so no network table at all | dominant everywhere |
| **Detection precision** | ATH-004 fires on `lsass.exe` itself: 30/day on a 30-host estate; ATH-005 fires HIGH with no success 35/36 times; K8S-001 fires on every legitimate `cluster-admin` binding, 2,070/day on CI | second, and the direct cause of analyst load |
| **Triage** | 0% reduction on every real dataset: 124 of 124 findings across flaws.cloud, Kubernetes CI and both DEDALE days were HIGH+ and the benign layer defers at HIGH | third, structural rather than a signal gap |
| **Detection coverage** | T1036 masquerading and T1547.001 Run-key persistence representable and undescribed; AWS-001 chain absent or mis-windowed | fourth |
| **Reasoning** | Could not be measured for the LLM arm (no key). Deterministic investigation on the one case that formed: 5 claims, 0 rejected, 0 fabricated, all cited, on a false positive | no evidence of failure; no evidence of value either |

The deterministic-versus-LLM question this milestone set out to answer is **not answerable
from this machine**: the LLM arm exists, is wired into the benchmark, and never ran. What
the data does say is that on real telemetry the investigation stage almost never gets a
true positive to reason about, because the layers before it either cannot see the attack
or bury it under boot noise. Improving the agent would improve nothing measurable until
that changes.

**M15 proposal, from the measured failures only** (in the order the evidence ranks them;
none of this is remediation done here):

1. *Representation, endpoint:* a canonical home for PowerShell script-block / module
   logging (4103/4104) and a decision on Sysmon 10 process-access and 22 DNS, driven by
   which labelled DEDALE events they would resolve (9,483 currently unresolvable). Measure
   by re-running the D15 incident: resolved refs and recall must move.
2. *Representation, control plane:* `serviceaccounts/token`, `secrets` reads and IAM
   read/describe families as control rows, so the Kubernetes and AWS rows stop being 3%
   ingestion. Measure by unmapped fraction and by whether K8S-002-style chains gain the
   token-then-exec step.
3. *Detection precision:* ATH-004 must not match the image path of `lsass.exe` itself;
   ATH-005's severity must depend on its own success condition; K8S-001 needs a
   prevalence term ("is a cluster-admin grant normal here") like ATH-006 already has.
   Measure by the benign-day false-positive counts (30/day, 35/36, 2,070/day) going to
   zero without the labelled true positives moving.
4. *Triage:* the HIGH-severity veto makes the layer inert on three of four real sources.
   Whether it should defer at HIGH is the design question; the measurement to settle it is
   how many of the 124 vetoed findings a benign signal would have explained.
5. *Detection coverage:* T1036 (executable from user-writable path with a system-like
   name, unsigned) and T1547.001 (Run-key write), the two representable, undescribed
   behaviours in the DEDALE chain; LibreOffice in the Office-application set.
6. *Then, and only then, the LLM arm,* on a machine with a key, on the DEDALE days
   where by then at least one true-positive case forms. Three runs, the 5.1 criteria.

Unavailable and carried forward unchanged: Kubernetes attack injection (needs Docker),
BOTS v3 (needs Splunk), every LLM row.

## Changes to the codebase made for this milestone

- `CloudTrailSource` reads `*.json`, `*.json.gz` and `*.tar` bundles in place, and falls
  back to `userIdentity.invokedBy` for service-invoked calls. Tests:
  `tests/test_cloudtrail_inputs.py`.
- `K8sAuditSource` reads NDJSON (`*.log`, `*.jsonl`) as well as EventLists; maps
  `get`-verb execs and 101 responses; canonicalises exec rows to verb `exec` and
  ServiceAccount subjects to the audit username form. K8S-002 matches on `exec`. Tests:
  `tests/test_k8s_audit_inputs.py`, `tests/test_k8s_exec_mapping.py`.
- `ath.evaluation.external_labels`: native-id labels resolved through `source_ref`;
  unresolved and ambiguous refs are counted. A test asserts no module outside
  `ath.evaluation` imports it. Tests: `tests/test_external_labels.py`.
- `run_incident` / `run_benchmark` accept an `llm` and `InvestigationConfig`;
  `IncidentOutcome.configuration` names the arm and `llm_degraded` flags a fallback.
  `python main.py benchmark --llm`. Tests: `tests/test_incident_llm_arm.py`.
- `ath.evaluation.profile`: label-free measurement of hunt, triage and correlation with
  per-stage timings. Tests: `tests/test_profile.py`.
- `ath.telemetry.winlogbeat_source`: Winlogbeat/ECS adapter (Sysmon 1/3, Security 4624/4625), with
  per-class unmapped counting added to `SourceLoadResult`. Tests: `tests/test_winlogbeat_source.py`.
- `scripts/fetch_external.py` (manifest-driven fetch with checksums, zip-range for DEDALE),
  `scripts/dedale_fetch_hours.py` (range-fetch, filter and count DEDALE hours), `scripts/dedale_labels.py`
  (DEDALE labels to native refs), `scripts/m14_profile.py` (one row per unlabelled dataset) and
  `scripts/m14_incident.py` (labelled incident, both arms) and `scripts/m14_table.py` (the consolidated table
  from `reports/m14/*.json` plus `reports/m14/classification.json`).

No detection threshold, benign signal or agent was changed. One rule predicate changed
vocabulary (`K8S-002`: `create` to `exec`) to match the adapter's canonical verb; it
detects the same behaviour.
